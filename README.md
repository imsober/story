**My Story**

  

This is my personal story. It may be a bit lengthy, and I understand it might not be necessary to take up your valuable time, but I would be grateful for any time you can spare.

  

**A Quiet Flame**

I was born into a hustling, middle-class Nepali family, the kind that knows the meaning of “enough” but always dreams of more. My father, a strict and principled teacher, and my mother, a gentle lady who ran a small stationery and bookshop, formed the backbone of my childhood. I was their first child, a shy, bookish boy who learned to read stories well above his grade because, unlike most, I had access to all the books I could dream of. That bookshop, small and cluttered, was my world.

  

Mom shaped my early years. She sent me to a better school, to private tutors, to cousins’ houses to learn, and to her parents where I got love in its purest form. She had high hopes, the kind that is above the grades and dreams beyond borders. My younger brother and sister didn’t quite get the same attention. It wasn’t favoritism, maybe it was just the reality of how firstborns are often seen.

  

My grandfather, a man who lived like a monk, would carry me on his shoulders, and show me around the beautiful hills, rivers and rice fields. "Don’t be discouraged in sorrow, and don’t get carried away in happiness," my grandmother used to say. Those words have echoed throughout my life.

  

**A Dreamer**

I was never the loudest in the room, never the center of attention, but I was focused. I earned my way into one of the best engineering colleges in Nepal, often regarded as the top institute of my country. I didn’t demand much from my parents. I lived within my means, quietly pushing my limits.

  

Right after college, four friends and I started a real estate marketplace startup. It failed terribly. All the brainstorming and networking sessions eventually turned into boozing and partying. We were full of big talk, but no one did the actual work. Then all five ambitious people tried to move in different directions. Ultimately, our immaturity led to failure.

  

During that time, the hunger for innovation and building something was always there. However, I must concede that my effort was not steady. Around 2017–2018, I met one of my seniors from college who was running an e-commerce and fashion brand. We discussed building an application where customers could communicate with a chat agent to ask for and purchase items. He asked me to build a prototype. I worked on it for a couple of days ([Module link](https://github.com/chapagainmanoj/dialog_builder), [Module link2](https://github.com/chapagainmanoj/galaxy)) , but the motivation soon faded away.

  

After that, I focused on my career and worked for three years at a reputed company ([Khalti](https://khalti.com/)), then moved on to other roles. I kept learning and growing professionally, juggling new opportunities. While working at [Codavatar](https://codavatar.com/), I met a founder and investor — a charismatic man who promised us big dreams. He told me and several senior members of the team that he was going to build at least ten different high-class tech products, and that we would all be given major roles/equity in them. However, building world-class and impactful software is an enormous challenge, let alone succeeding with it. Although they provided a very positive environment for growth, after a couple of years, I felt I couldn’t keep waiting.

  

Then, I worked with a Singapore-based startup ([MicroSec](https://www.microsec.io/)) and a Finland-based team ([Resimator](https://resimator.fi/en)). For a while, life was good, not glamorous, but peaceful. I was earning well, supporting my parents, traveling between Pokhara (hometown), Kathmandu, and places and indulging in small joys and adventures: beers by the lake, meals with friends, quiet walk, treks.

  
But there was a voice inside me that wanted more, to see the world, to break the ceiling I felt above me in Nepal. I always felt confident in my expertise and field, but communication was never my strength. I believed that for my overall personal growth and to reach the next milestone, I needed to get out of the country. My own backyard felt too small for my dreams.

  

My first attempt was to pursue a master's degree at a U.S. university. I took the GRE and did all the necessary research. Then COVID hit, and I decided to take a break and I never ended up applying to any university.

  
Later, I heard that one of my friends was going to Canada, and I thought, Why not Canada?

  

  

**The Cracks**

In our culture, we stay very close to our families. I am the sole breadwinner for my family. My brother is unemployed, and my parents no longer have any hopes for him. My sister is doing well enough on her own. My parents are getting older, and they want to see us married, earning well, and settled. Whatever little savings they have left will likely be spent on our marriages. Lately, my mother had been ill; she was taking medication for high blood pressure and heart problems. It’s what worries me a lot. During the final days before I left for Canada, I wasn’t feeling right from inside. I always felt like I was rushing through everything, and the signs weren’t good, kind of bad omen but I didn’t care much.

  

Canada hit me harder than I ever expected. The culture shock, the loneliness, the endless hustle it all came up to me fast. I finished all my savings and refused to ask my parents for more. I almost finished my one-year certificate course, but the dream I came with to grow my network, study, transition into a tech career, and slowly growing up began to slip away. For good jobs and good networking, I didn’t have sufficient communication skills or my attempts were not enough. Night shifts at a gas station job, pointless arguments with thiefs, meaningless hours washing dishes it all drained me. Surrounded by rowdy youngsters in early 20’s just starting their lives, I felt misplaced, a grown man pretending to start over. In the new place never found similar minded people. Home felt farther away with each passing day. I couldn’t manage the frustration and disappointment. I turned to alcohol, trying to numb the pain inside.

  

When I landed in Canada, I thought I had a plan: study, get a part-time work or freelance, get into tech culture, either land into big IT job or build somethings. But plans don't prepare you for rejection, for cold basements, for endless silence. I stopped reaching out to people. I stopped asking myself if I was okay. And then, just when I was beginning to break, she appeared.

  

  

  

**A Misunderstanding**

I met her(Smriti) in the middle of my chaos. She was young, vibrant, and had some aura. I wasn’t looking for romance, I was looking for someone to talk to. I didn’t know her age at the time, I thought she was older, maybe someone like me trying to figure things out, after first few times we didn’t talked much for long time. But suddenly she messaged me one day, then when I talked to her next day and she shared something that was very strange to me. She said she has no one to talk to, all she had was toys ( stuffed dolls). She showed me 10+ toys and told me they all have names and she keeps talking to them for hours.

  

What followed was a disaster the lowest point of my life.

  

I was arrested, handcuffed, thrown into a freezing cell, and surrounded by criminals, murderers, drug dealers, rapists. I was terrified. I lived five days in hell. I slept on cold concrete, dressed in an orange prison uniform, hands chained, my mind breaking. I thought about ending all but felt I should continue for those we love me.

  

In those dark corners, I revisited every memory my childhood, my family’s dreams, my mother’s love, my father's sacrifices, my own dreams. I wrote a poem on toilet paper with a short pencil. Even wrote a draft for a imaginative software.

  

After five days, I was released.

  

  

**Soul Searching**

  

But I wasn’t free.

For the months after my bail, I couldn’t stop thinking, caught in trauma, in fragments, in anxiety all the time. Always the unfinished thoughts and relentless self-questioning. I found myself in deep soul-searching. In the past, whenever I heard news about sexual abuse or rape, my blood would boil. I was one of the strongest critics of such shameful crimes. I believed the perpetrators deserved the harshest punishment, even to be hanged. And now, I can’t believe I find myself in this situation. The shame, the guilt, the weight of it crushes me.

  

I questioned my character my values my intentions. I picked apart every moment, trying to understand if I had crossed any line. And even if my heart said no, the consequences said otherwise. I stopped seeing myself as a victim of misfortune, I started seeing a man who had failed to act with more clarity, more maturity, more care.

  

After some time, I started working at a small company. I’m deeply thankful to my employer for their patience and trust in me. Even when I’ve been consumed by constant thoughts that affect my focus, they’ve given me the space and support to complete my tasks. I began doing the work I know and love. The cloud of bad omens started to lift. Slowly, I’m stitching myself back together — piece by piece. I dream again. I laugh sometimes. I’m trying to build something meaningful — a project, maybe even Journo, an itinerary management system I began drafting while still in custody.

  

I have learned the hard way that the life is a infinite game and it’s not about winning. It’s about (simply) staying in the game. Some days, I want to give up. Some nights, I cry alone. But I remember there are people who love me, who believe in me. I owe it to them, and to myself, to live, to keep going. Mental health is real. Situational anxiety is real. And sometimes, therapy is simply facing life one day at a time, knowing that the win is not just for today but tomorrow, and the day after.

  

I thought my life was over. That a single mistake, a collapse in judgment, a moment of disorientation, could swallow everything I’d built and everything I was meant to become. But pain also reveals what’s left And what I found was this: A man who could begin again. I don’t want to be that version of me again. Ever. I’m learning to surround myself with people who radiate positivity. I know there’s a lot I still need to improve. I sometimes present myself too naively I’m working on that. Beneath that  I know my value. I know my ambition. That I needed to grow, not just professionally, but emotionally. Spiritually. As a man.

  

To her, I have few thing to say:

I should’ve been more aware. More cautious. More grown up. You weren’t responsible for my mess.

I never meant to hurt you. I was broken, confused, lost. I hold no grudge. I just hope you're happy. That you’re safe. That you're growing. I hope none of this ever held you back.

I hope you’re surrounded by people who make you feel safe, seen, and strong.

Whatever it was, I’m sorry.

Truly.

  

This is real from my heart. I have wrote to her many times knowing I’ll never send. I know an apology may never be enough, but it’s all I could to offer.

  

  

  

**The Weight I Carry**

I haven’t shared much of this with my family yet. I think my dad might know a little, but he hasn’t discussed it with me yet. My mother is completely unaware of it all. I can’t imagine how she could possibly bear the news that her son is in jail on a sexual assault charge in a foreign country, in Canada. My biggest fear is that it would break her that her heart would simply explode.

  

I talk to her at least once a week and send money to my family on every payday. My father usually asks for it on the first day of each month because he believes I get my salary at the end of the month. Both of my parents are retired now, and my father’s government pension is very low. Mother sold her small book shop very long ago. My brother doesn’t do anything. My sister used to work, but she recently left her job. I know she could at least earn something for herself or even contribute a little to the family, but overall, I am the one they rely on for a significant amount of support.

  

This setback will surely hamper my future career. It will completely destroy my future and my IT career. Almost every company does criminal background checks.

Likewise, the doors to most countries will be closed for life, not just to me, but to my family as well. Which would be even more devastating. I still don’t know if I’ll ever travel again. Or even worse.

  

It takes years to build and seconds to destroy it. But I will continue to build something again. Not just software. Myself. I used to think success was about achievement degrees, salaries, visas, startups. But real success? It’s walking through the fire and not letting it turn you into someone bitter. It’s holding onto your humanity, even when the everything tries to squeeze it out of you.

**Sharing Kindness Quietly**

**Samaanta Foundation**  
Samaanta Foundation is a philanthropic organization that provides education to disadvantaged communities. _Samaanta_ itself means "equality" in Nepali.

In 2022, I met a 16-year-old girl named Dila KC, a brave young woman raised by a single mother, as her father had passed away. Coming from a very poor background, Dila told me about the foundation that was helping provide education for young boys and girls like her. At the time, she was raising funds for her batch as they graduated from grade 10 to 11.

I was deeply impressed by her courage, ambition, and dedication to building a better future. I'm proud and grateful that even the small contribution I was able to make is going toward something so meaningful.

**NRNA Canada**

NRNA has played a significant role for anyone living in Canada, especially for people from Nepal and South Asia. The cultural shock can be overwhelming, and not everyone adapts quickly to life so far from home. I’ve seen many young people struggle with mental health and other challenges, many of them feeling lost, helpless and alone.

  

Giving back to the community, to the future, has become one of my quiet promises to myself. I’ve been through enough to know that everyone is carrying something heavy. And if my journey full of mistakes, missteps, and moments of grace can offer even a small hope or inspiration to someone else, then maybe it was all worth it.

I’m not done giving. Not yet. And definitely not alone.

  

Thank you from the bottom of my heart for taking the time to read this. Whatever you decide, I will respect it fully and carry it with grace as I move forward.
